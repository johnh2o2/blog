## Abstract

Template periodograms extend the Lomb-Scargle periodogram by fitting an arbitrary periodic template — rather than a sinusoid — at each trial frequency. This is critical for detecting non-sinusoidal periodic signals (eclipsing binaries, RR Lyrae, Cepheids) that are poorly characterized by sinusoidal models.

The standard template periodogram requires O(N²) operations and relies on non-linear optimization at each trial frequency. Because the phase landscape has multiple local minima, gradient-based solvers frequently converge to suboptimal solutions. We present a new algorithm that reduces the complexity to **O(N log N)** while **guaranteeing the global optimum** at every trial frequency.

## Method

The template periodogram statistic measures the improvement in fit over the mean model:

$$
P(\omega) = \frac{\chi^2_0 - \chi^2(\omega)}{\chi^2_0}
$$

For a fixed phase $\theta_2$, amplitude and offset have closed-form solutions, reducing the fit to a 1D optimization over phase. The algorithm proceeds in two stages:

**1. NUFFT for all-frequency sums.** Represent the template as $H$ Fourier harmonics. The weighted data–harmonic covariances needed for fitting can be computed at all trial frequencies simultaneously via Non-Uniform FFTs in $O(H \cdot N_f \log N_f)$ time.

**2. Polynomial zero-finding for optimal phase.** The substitution $\psi = e^{i\theta_2}$ converts the trigonometric optimality condition into a polynomial equation of degree $6H - 1$:

$$
2\,\text{MM}(\psi)\,\text{YM}'(\psi) - \text{MM}'(\psi)\,\text{YM}(\psi) = 0
$$

where $\text{YM}$ (degree $2H$) encodes data–template covariance and $\text{MM}$ (degree $4H$) encodes template self-covariance. Finding all roots enumerates every critical point of $P(\theta_2)$, guaranteeing the global maximum — no local minimum can be missed.

This makes template-based period searches feasible for modern time-domain surveys with millions of light curves.

## Impact

The algorithm and its [open-source implementation](https://github.com/johnh2o2/fast-template-periodogram) enable survey-scale searches for non-sinusoidal periodic variables — a capability previously limited to small, targeted studies.
