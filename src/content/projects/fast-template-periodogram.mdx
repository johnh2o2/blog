## Overview

The **Fast Template Periodogram** is a novel algorithm that reduces the computational complexity of template periodograms from O(N^2) to O(N log N), making template-based period searches feasible at survey scale.

Traditional template periodograms fit an arbitrary periodic template — not just a sinusoid — at each trial frequency. This is essential for detecting non-sinusoidal signals like eclipsing binaries or RR Lyrae stars, where a sinusoidal model misses most of the signal power. The standard approach requires solving a non-linear least squares problem at every trial frequency: optimizing amplitude, offset, and phase shift. The phase optimization is particularly problematic — the objective has multiple local minima, so gradient-based solvers frequently miss the global optimum.

## The Algorithm

The algorithm has two key insights that work together:

**1. NUFFT for all-frequency sums.** The template is represented as a truncated Fourier series with $H$ harmonics. The weighted sums needed for fitting — covariances between the data and the harmonic basis functions — can be expressed as Non-Uniform FFTs and evaluated at all trial frequencies simultaneously in $O(H \cdot N_f \log N_f)$ time.

**2. Polynomial zero-finding for globally optimal phase.** At each trial frequency, amplitude and offset have closed-form solutions given the phase $\theta_2$, reducing the fit to a 1D optimization. The substitution $\psi = e^{i\theta_2}$ converts the trigonometric expressions in $\theta_2$ into polynomials in $\psi$. The optimality condition $dP/d\theta_2 = 0$ becomes a polynomial of degree $6H - 1$:

$$
2\,\text{MM}(\psi)\,\text{YM}'(\psi) - \text{MM}'(\psi)\,\text{YM}(\psi) = 0
$$

where $\text{YM}(\psi)$ (degree $2H$) encodes the data–template covariance and $\text{MM}(\psi)$ (degree $4H$) encodes the template self-covariance. Finding *all* roots of this polynomial and evaluating the periodogram power at each one **guarantees the global optimum** — every critical point is enumerated, so no local minimum can be missed.

This combination makes template-based period searches feasible for surveys like ZTF and LSST with millions of light curves.
