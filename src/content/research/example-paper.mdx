## Abstract

We develop and deploy a convolutional neural network architecture for automated classification of stellar spectra from the Sloan Digital Sky Survey (SDSS). Our method achieves 98.7% accuracy across seven spectral types (O, B, A, F, G, K, M), representing a significant improvement over traditional template-matching approaches.

## Introduction

The exponential growth in astronomical survey data presents both opportunities and challenges. Modern spectroscopic surveys like SDSS, LAMOST, and GALAH generate millions of stellar spectra, far exceeding the capacity for manual classification. Traditional methods rely on template matching or principal component analysis, but these approaches struggle with noisy data and atypical spectra.

Machine learning offers a promising alternative. Recent work has demonstrated the viability of neural networks for spectral classification, but performance has been limited by:

1. Insufficient training data with reliable labels
2. Model architectures not optimized for spectroscopic data
3. Poor generalization to spectra outside the training distribution

Our work addresses these limitations through careful dataset curation, architectural innovations, and systematic evaluation on held-out test sets.

## Methods

### Dataset Construction

We curated a labeled dataset of 450,000 SDSS spectra spanning:

- **Spectral types**: O, B, A, F, G, K, M
- **Signal-to-noise ratios**: 5 to 100
- **Metallicities**: $-2.5 < [\text{Fe/H}] < +0.5$
- **Surface gravities**: $3.5 < \log g < 5.0$

Labels were derived from SSPP (SEGUE Stellar Parameter Pipeline) outputs, manually validated for a 10,000-spectrum subset.

### Neural Network Architecture

Our model consists of:

1. **Input normalization**: Continuum normalization and wavelength standardization
2. **1D Convolutional layers**: Extract spectral features (line depths, widths, profiles)
3. **Attention mechanism**: Weight informative spectral regions
4. **Classification head**: Softmax over seven stellar types

The architecture processes spectra at native SDSS resolution (3800 wavelength bins) without dimensionality reduction.

### Training Procedure

We employed standard supervised learning with cross-entropy loss:

$$
\mathcal{L} = -\sum_{i=1}^{N} \sum_{c=1}^{C} y_{ic} \log(\hat{y}_{ic})
$$

where $y_{ic}$ are true labels and $\hat{y}_{ic}$ are predicted probabilities for class $c$ and spectrum $i$.

Training used:
- **Optimizer**: Adam with $\beta_1 = 0.9$, $\beta_2 = 0.999$
- **Learning rate**: $10^{-4}$ with cosine annealing
- **Batch size**: 128
- **Regularization**: Dropout (0.3) and weight decay ($10^{-5}$)

## Results

### Classification Performance

On the held-out test set (50,000 spectra), our model achieves:

- **Overall accuracy**: 98.7%
- **Macro-averaged F1**: 0.984
- **Per-class precision**: >97% for all spectral types

Performance degrades gracefully with decreasing S/N, maintaining >95% accuracy even at S/N = 10.

### Comparison to Baselines

Our approach outperforms:
- Template matching: 92.3% accuracy
- Random Forest: 95.8% accuracy
- Previous CNN (Smith et al. 2023): 96.4% accuracy

### Attention Analysis

Visualization of attention weights reveals the model focuses on:
- Balmer lines (H$\alpha$, H$\beta$) for early-type stars
- Metal lines (Ca II, Mg I) for late-type stars
- TiO bands for M-type stars

This aligns with physical intuition and traditional classification criteria.

## Discussion

Our results demonstrate that deep learning can automate stellar spectral classification with accuracy exceeding traditional methods. The attention mechanism provides interpretability, revealing which spectral features drive classification decisions.

**Limitations**: Performance on rare spectral types (e.g., white dwarfs, carbon stars) is untested. Future work should expand to more exotic stellar populations.

**Deployment**: We provide pre-trained models and inference code for community use. The system processes 1000 spectra per second on a single GPU, enabling real-time classification for upcoming surveys like DESI.

## Conclusion

We present a deep learning system for stellar spectral classification achieving state-of-the-art accuracy. Our work demonstrates the maturity of machine learning for astronomical applications and provides a foundation for future research in automated data analysis.
