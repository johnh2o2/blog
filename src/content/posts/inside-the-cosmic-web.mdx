The cosmic web takes shape around you. Gas gathers into filaments, dense knots
grow, and feedback from stars and black holes changes their surroundings.
I made a 9 minute 45 second film of this history from the IllustrisTNG simulation,
with a fixed viewpoint and a full sphere of sky to explore. Figure 1 shows one
view from the film.

The player opens separately. On a computer or phone, press **Play film** and
drag to look around. On a headset, open the same link in its browser and choose
**Enter 360°** when available. You can turn your head in any direction; moving
sideways does not change the perspective. The film is monoscopic, with no
positional parallax.

Music starts with the film. Brian’s optional AI narration is off by default;
**Sound & text** lets you add the voice, adjust the music, or hide the labels.
Playback follows the authored pace. The 4K and 8K options contain the same film;
4K is easier to decode, but both use roughly 0.8 GB for a complete viewing.
A reliable broadband connection helps. If streaming does not work in your
browser, **Color, scale & source → Try MP4 playback** uses the original movie.

## A History From One Place

The film covers cosmic ages from about 49 million to 13.8 billion years,
using all 4,380 native outputs of TNG100-2 Subbox 0. The region is 11.1 comoving
megaparsecs across, or about 36 million light-years at today’s scale. The camera
stays at its center in comoving coordinates, which follow the overall expansion
of the universe.

Each second spans about 23.5 million years. Between saved outputs, gas positions
are interpolated using persistent cell IDs and recorded velocities; cells that
appear or disappear fade locally. This is a simultaneous view of a simulated
volume, rather than a telescope observation that mixes different light-travel
times along a line of sight.

## Reading the Film

Brightness represents the amount of gas integrated along each viewing direction.
Color represents its column-density-weighted temperature: blue toward the cool
end, through cyan to amber and red at higher temperatures. Dense cores also
become pale. These are scientific false colors, with fixed logarithmic keys
in the player, rather than visible-light emission. Temperatures are approximate,
particularly for the model’s star-forming gas.

Sparse annotations locate three measured stellar-wind regions, two black holes
with recorded thermal feedback, and two catalogued galaxies. Later, a distance
annotation gives the three-dimensional separation of two measured centers.
The simulation models collective supernova-driven winds; it does not resolve
individual supernova explosions. A pointer identifies a measured source region,
not the boundary or unique cause of every visible puff.

The age clock and era titles supply broad cosmological context. Those chapter
boundaries are approximate, not detections of the first stars or galaxies in
this particular volume. The player includes the
[complete text, timings, measurement notes, and simulation credits](https://cosmic-web-film.cosmology-lab-ac48dc.workers.dev/film-text.html).
